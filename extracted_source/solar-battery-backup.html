How to Calculate Solar Battery Backup Time

Published September 2026 · Practical technology guide
Quick answerEstimate backup time by converting battery capacity to energy and dividing usable energy by the load, then account for inverter losses and the battery's recommended usable discharge. For a simple nominal estimate: runtime hours ≈ (V × Ah × usable fraction × efficiency) ÷ load watts.

Worked example

  • A nominal 12 V, 100 Ah battery contains about 1,200 Wh before practical limits and losses are considered.
  • If a system used 80% of that nominal energy and the inverter delivered 90% efficiency, the rough usable AC energy would be 1,200 × 0.8 × 0.9 = 864 Wh.
  • At a steady 200 W load, that simplified estimate would be about 864 ÷ 200 = 4.32 hours.
  • Real runtime can differ because battery capacity varies with discharge rate, battery condition, temperature, wiring losses, and the actual load over time. Use manufacturer specifications for final system design.

Battery backup time depends on usable battery energy, inverter efficiency, and the actual load. A quick voltage × amp-hour calculation is useful for estimating capacity, but it should not be treated as the exact runtime.

Important: Lead-acid batteries generally should not be fully discharged. Use the manufacturer's recommended depth of discharge when making a practical estimate.

Step 1: Estimate battery energy

For a nominal battery, multiply voltage by amp-hours. For example, a 12 V, 100 Ah battery has a nominal energy of about 1,200 Wh.

Step 2: Account for usable capacity

If you plan to use only 50% of the nominal capacity, the usable energy is roughly 600 Wh. The real figure can be lower because battery capacity changes with discharge rate, temperature, age, and battery condition.

Step 3: Account for the load

If your appliances consume 200 W continuously, dividing 600 Wh by 200 W gives a theoretical 3 hours before inverter losses and other factors.

A better way to estimate runtime

  • Measure the actual AC or DC load if possible.
  • Use the battery manufacturer's discharge specifications.
  • Include inverter efficiency when powering AC appliances.
  • Allow for battery ageing and temperature.

Common mistake

Do not assume a 100 Ah battery always delivers exactly 100 Ah at every load. The rating is measured under specified test conditions.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using the battery's full rated Wh as guaranteed usable energy.
  • Ignoring inverter efficiency and the power drawn by the load itself.
  • Estimating runtime from appliance wattage without checking startup or changing loads.
  • Mixing 12 V and 24 V figures in the same calculation.

Questions to check before you finish

What is the basic runtime formula?

A useful planning estimate is battery voltage × rated Ah × usable fraction × inverter efficiency, divided by load watts.

Why is actual runtime shorter than the simple calculation?

Rated capacity is measured under defined conditions. Real runtime also depends on discharge rate, battery condition, temperature, wiring, conversion losses, and changing loads.

Should I size a battery from the inverter's maximum rating?

Size the battery around the actual expected load and required runtime, then verify that the inverter and battery can safely handle continuous and surge demand.
