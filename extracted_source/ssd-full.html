SSD Storage Nearly Full: What to Delete Safely

Published September 2026 · Practical technology guide
Quick answerWhen an SSD is nearly full, start by locating the largest folders and files. Clear safe temporary data, remove unused applications, and move or delete personal files you no longer need. Avoid deleting system files simply because they are large.

Safe cleanup sequence

  • Use the operating system's storage breakdown to identify applications, temporary files, documents, media, and other categories.
  • Empty recycle/trash locations only after checking their contents.
  • Review large downloads, installers, old videos, virtual-machine images, and game libraries if they are no longer needed.
  • Leave room for normal operating-system activity and updates rather than filling the drive to its last few megabytes.

Start with the largest files

Use Windows storage settings to find large apps, temporary files, videos, downloads, and other categories. Remove files you can identify rather than guessing.

Temporary files

Windows provides built-in cleanup options. Review the categories before confirming deletion because some items may include files you still need.

Move large personal files

Photos, videos, project archives, and installers can often be moved to another drive or backed-up storage.

Do not chase every last gigabyte

An SSD benefits from having some free space for normal operation. The exact amount varies by workload and drive.

Keep backups

Deleting a file to free space is not a backup strategy. Keep important data in at least one additional location.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Deleting Windows or application folders manually to reclaim space.
  • Treating temporary files as the only cause of a nearly full drive.
  • Moving programs or system folders without using supported migration methods.
  • Leaving almost no free space and then diagnosing slowdowns as a CPU problem.

Questions to check before you finish

How much free space should I leave on an SSD?

There is no single universal percentage, but keeping meaningful free space avoids a drive becoming completely constrained. The practical amount depends on capacity and workload.

What can I safely remove first?

Start with Windows storage recommendations, temporary files, unused applications, large downloads, and other files you can identify confidently.

Can a full SSD make a PC feel slow?

It can. Low free space can interfere with temporary files and normal system operations, although CPU, memory, thermals, and other factors may also be responsible.
