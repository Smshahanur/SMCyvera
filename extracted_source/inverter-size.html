How to Calculate the Right Inverter Size

Published September 2026 · Practical technology guide
Quick answerSize an inverter from the loads you actually expect to run at the same time, while allowing for startup surges from motors and other equipment. The inverter's continuous rating, surge rating, battery voltage, and wiring limits all matter.

Worked load example

  • Suppose a system may run three 60 W fans and three 10 W lights together: the running load is about 210 W before any other devices or losses.
  • A motor-driven appliance can briefly demand much more power when starting, so its nameplate running wattage alone may not be enough for inverter selection.
  • Add the loads that can genuinely overlap rather than simply adding every appliance in the house.
  • Check the inverter's recommended battery voltage and maximum current, and size cables and protection for the actual DC current.

List the loads

Write down the wattage of every appliance that may operate simultaneously. Nameplate wattage is a starting point.

Account for startup surge

Motors, pumps, refrigerators, and some power tools can require substantially more power when starting.

Add a practical margin

Choose an inverter that can handle the continuous load without operating at its absolute limit.

Check battery-side current

A large AC load on a low-voltage battery can require high DC current. Cable size, fusing, connectors, and battery capability matter.

Match the waveform and voltage

Verify battery voltage and output requirements. Sensitive equipment may have specific requirements; use the manufacturer's recommendations.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Sizing the inverter only from the sum of normal running watts.
  • Ignoring motor or pump startup surge.
  • Selecting an inverter whose DC voltage does not match the battery bank.
  • Using a high-power appliance beyond the battery, wiring, or inverter's continuous limits.

Questions to check before you finish

How do I calculate inverter running watts?

Add the normal running power of the loads you expect to use simultaneously, then allow appropriate headroom rather than sizing exactly to the total.

Why do pumps and motors need extra capacity?

Their starting current can be much higher than their normal running demand. The inverter must support the required surge for the expected duration.

Does inverter size determine battery runtime?

No. Inverter size describes what the inverter can supply; battery runtime depends mainly on stored usable energy, load, and conversion losses.
