Battery Ah vs Wh Explained

Published September 2026 · Practical technology guide
Quick answerAmp-hours describe charge capacity relative to a stated voltage, while watt-hours express energy. For a simple nominal conversion, multiply volts by amp-hours: Wh = V × Ah. Real usable energy can be lower because of losses, discharge limits, temperature, and battery characteristics.

Worked example: 12 V and 100 Ah

  • A nominal 12 V, 100 Ah battery has 12 × 100 = 1,200 Wh of nominal energy.
  • That does not mean a 1,200 W load will run for exactly one hour. Inverter losses and the battery's usable discharge range change the practical result.
  • For comparisons, convert batteries to watt-hours only after checking that the voltage rating is being compared on the same basis.
  • For an actual solar or backup system, use the battery manufacturer's specifications and the inverter's efficiency rather than relying only on the nominal calculation.

Amp-hours are not energy by themselves

Ah describes charge capacity at a stated voltage and test condition. To estimate energy, voltage must also be considered.

Basic conversion

Nominal watt-hours can be estimated by multiplying nominal volts by amp-hours.

Why real energy differs

Usable energy depends on battery chemistry, discharge rate, temperature, age, and the permitted depth of discharge.

Compare batteries correctly

When comparing batteries at different voltages, Wh is often more useful than Ah because it accounts for voltage.

For inverter systems

Include inverter efficiency and other losses when estimating how much AC energy a battery can actually deliver.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Confusing Ah with energy: Ah is a charge-capacity rating, while Wh also accounts for voltage.
  • Comparing batteries with different voltages using Ah alone.
  • Using the nameplate Ah as if the full amount is always usable.
  • Ignoring inverter or converter losses when estimating usable energy.

Questions to check before you finish

What does Ah tell me?

Ah describes how much electrical charge a battery is rated to store at a stated test condition; it is not a direct energy figure unless voltage is also known.

How do I convert Ah to Wh?

A simple planning calculation is Wh = volts × Ah. For example, a 12 V, 100 Ah battery is rated at about 1,200 Wh before accounting for usable capacity and losses.

Why can two 100 Ah batteries provide different energy?

If their nominal voltages differ, their watt-hours differ. Chemistry, discharge conditions, and usable depth of discharge also affect practical energy.
