How do I calculate how long a battery will run an appliance?

Theoretical battery runtime is calculated by multiplying battery voltage (V) by rated capacity (Ah), multiplying by depth-of-discharge and inverter efficiency (typically 85–90%), and dividing by continuous load wattage (W). For example, a 12V 100Ah battery at 85% usable efficiency running a 150W load provides approximately 6.8 hours of power.

Why is Android storage still full after deleting photos and apps?

Deleted files often remain in the "Trash" or "Recently Deleted" albums for 30 days before storage is reclaimed. App cache accumulations, messaging app media backups (e.g., WhatsApp databases), and temporary system download logs also occupy invisible space until cleared in device storage settings.

Read Android storage guide →

How can I improve home Wi-Fi coverage across dead zones?

Begin by elevating your primary router to a central, open location away from concrete walls and large metal appliances. Separate network bands: connect nearby devices to 5 GHz for maximum speed, and distant devices to 2.4 GHz for superior wall penetration. If dead zones persist, installing a wired access point provides lower latency than repeaters.

Read Wi-Fi coverage guide →

What is the difference between MPPT and PWM solar charge controllers?

PWM controllers force solar panel voltage down to battery voltage, clipping off excess voltage and losing that potential energy. MPPT controllers electronically convert excess panel voltage into additional charging current, delivering 15% to 30% more energy into your battery bank, especially during cold or overcast conditions.

Read MPPT vs PWM guide →

Should I upgrade my computer to an SSD or continue using an HDD?

Upgrading from an HDD to a Solid State Drive (SSD) is the single most impactful performance upgrade for a PC. SSDs deliver 4× to 30× faster read/write speeds, near-instant operating system boot times, and silent durability without moving mechanical parts. HDDs are now only recommended for bulk archival storage.

Read SSD vs HDD guide →

What is the difference between battery Amp-hours (Ah) and Watt-hours (Wh)?

Amp-hours (Ah) measure electrical charge capacity at a specific nominal voltage, while Watt-hours (Wh) measure total stored electrical energy regardless of voltage (Wh = Ah × V). Comparing batteries using Watt-hours enables direct, apples-to-apples comparisons across different system voltages.
