How to Back Up Android Before a Factory Reset

By Shahanur Mollah · Published September 2026 · Updated October 2026
Quick answerBefore a factory reset, back up the data you cannot replace: photos, contacts, messages where supported, files, authenticator/recovery information, and app data that matters to you. Then verify that the backup can actually be accessed before resetting the phone.

A practical backup checklist

  • Check the phone's account backup status and the last successful backup time. A backup that is still running is not a completed backup.
  • Open your photo/file backup service and confirm that recent photos and important folders are present. Do not assume every folder is included automatically.
  • Export or record anything that depends on the phone itself, such as two-factor authentication recovery codes, before erasing it.
  • After the reset, restore only what you need first. This makes it easier to identify an app that causes a problem after restoration.

Why backup comes first

A factory reset removes locally stored data. Cloud synchronization does not automatically guarantee that every file, app setting, or account has been backed up.

Check your Google backup

Review the backup section in Settings and confirm that your important account data has synchronized. Menu names differ between Android versions.

Protect photos and videos

Open your photo backup service and confirm recent items are synchronized. If a file exists only on the phone, copy it to another safe location before resetting.

Secure authentication access

Make sure you can sign in again after the reset. Save recovery methods for important accounts and consider how authenticator apps or passkeys are restored.

After the reset

During setup, sign in to the correct account and restore only what you need. Reinstall important apps and verify critical files before treating the migration as complete.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Starting a factory reset before confirming that important photos, contacts, and files are actually backed up.
  • Assuming a cloud backup contains every app's local data.
  • Forgetting account credentials that may be required after the reset.
  • Backing up to the same phone storage that will be erased.

Questions to check before you finish

What should I back up before a factory reset?

Check photos, videos, contacts, messages where applicable, documents, authenticator data, app-specific files, and anything stored only in local phone storage.

How can I verify a backup?

Open the relevant cloud or computer backup and confirm that recent important files are actually visible or recoverable rather than assuming the backup completed.

Will a factory reset remove the phone's accounts?

It erases local device data and settings. Afterward, the phone may require the previously used account credentials as part of theft-protection measures.
