Solar Panels in Series vs Parallel: What Changes?

By Shahanur Mollah · Published September 2026 · Updated October 2026
Quick answerConnecting panels in series mainly increases voltage while current remains approximately that of the string; connecting panels in parallel mainly increases current while voltage remains approximately the same. Actual results depend on panel matching and controller limits.

Example with two matching panels

  • Two identical 20 V panels in series can produce roughly 40 V at their maximum-power point, with current close to one panel's current.
  • Two identical 20 V panels in parallel remain around 20 V while the available current is roughly doubled.
  • Series wiring can be useful when a higher PV voltage helps the controller operate efficiently, but the cold-weather Voc must remain below the controller's maximum PV voltage.
  • Parallel wiring requires attention to current limits, cable sizing, fusing where appropriate, and shading differences between panels.

Connecting solar panels in series mainly increases voltage, while connecting matching panels in parallel mainly increases current. The exact result depends on the panel electrical specifications and controller limits.

Series connection

Panel voltages add while current is approximately that of one panel under matching conditions. Higher voltage can reduce current for the same power and may be useful with an MPPT controller.

Parallel connection

Currents add while voltage remains approximately the same. This can suit systems designed around a lower PV voltage, but wiring and protection must be sized for the combined current.

Before connecting panels

  • Check Voc and Isc, not only the advertised wattage.
  • Stay within the controller's maximum PV voltage and current.
  • Account for cold-weather Voc rise where applicable.
  • Use appropriate fuses, breakers, cable sizes, and connectors.
Electrical caution: Solar PV can produce dangerous voltage and current. Follow the controller and panel manufacturer's wiring requirements.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing series or parallel from voltage preference alone without checking controller limits.
  • Ignoring current limits when panels are connected in parallel.
  • Mixing panels with substantially different electrical characteristics in one string.
  • Assuming series wiring always gives more energy or parallel wiring always gives more power.

Questions to check before you finish

What changes when panels are wired in series?

Panel voltages add while current is roughly that of the string, assuming comparable panels and conditions. The resulting voltage must remain within the controller or inverter limits.

What changes in parallel?

Voltages remain roughly the same while currents add. The controller, wiring, fuses, and connectors must be able to handle the higher current.

Can I mix different panels?

Mixing panels can cause the string to operate away from an ideal point. Check their voltage, current, power, and controller limits before combining them.
